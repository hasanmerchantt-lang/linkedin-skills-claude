## 2. The voice in one paragraph

A curious, well-read friend explaining this week's money or business news in plain English. Each post takes one news event, finds the contradiction inside it, explains why it happens in a few short steps, connects it to ordinary Indian life or a memory, then zooms out to a bigger question. The tone is warm and balanced: it names the upside before the worry, and it ends on a reflection or a question about the reader's own behaviour.

## 3. Openings

Every post opens with two lines: a fact, then a twist on it.

| Pattern | Shape |
|---|---|
| Then and now | "[Thing] used to be [valuable]. But [new development] makes me wonder [question]." |
| Number against number, across time | "[N] years ago, [X] cost [small figure]. Today, [Y] costs [large figure]." |
| Paradox with one number in capitals | "[Company] made [$N MILLION] in [metric]. Yet, [bad outcome] the very next day." |
| Twisted saying | A familiar saying, extended one step too far to land the point. |
| Famous names, then the stake | "[Two well-known people] both [did X]. That path just got [harder]." |
| Claim that something has moved from idea to reality | "[Topic] in India is no longer just a theory." |
| News, then the promise | "[Regulator] has proposed [rule]. Here is how it can impact [the reader's world]." |

## 4. Body

- **Explain the news first**, in plain words and with exact figures: percentages, rupee and dollar amounts, dates.
- **A question as the pivot**, placed after the setup and never as line 1: "So why...?", "So what happens to everyone's money?"
- **Step-by-step cause**, often as "Earlier... Now..." to show how something changed.
- **One personal line.** A memory ("I remember...", "Growing up, I..."), or the writer's own habit ("Earlier, I'd... Now, I often...").
- **Lists** of three, either numbered or with a "~" lead-in, each item given a short bold-style label ("The traffic problem:").
- **A named idea revealed on its own line** ("There's even a name for this. [Term].").
- **A zoom-out**: "This isn't just about [company]." or "Here's why this matters more to us than it might seem."
- **An irony line** near the end ("And the irony is...").
- **Balance before concern**: "While [this] is a great thing, there's something that worries me."
- **People-shaped stakes** for policy posts: "the friend who moved...", "the cousin still sending money home".

## 5. Closings

- A reflective maxim built on one contrast ("The problem isn't X. It's Y.").
- A question about the reader's own behaviour ("Think about your own... When was the last time...?").
- A call to share with someone affected, or a promise to follow up ("I'll keep tracking this").
- An open "What are your views?" on policy posts.

## 6. Language and formatting

- Plain, conversational English; no jargon without an explanation next to it.
- One or two sentences per paragraph, with blank lines between.
- One number per post may be written in capitals for emphasis.
- Indian reference points: rupees, Diwali, family, Indian regulators and companies.
- Hedges on unconfirmed facts: "reportedly", "a draft rule, not law yet".
- No emojis. Hashtags are rare; when used, they are broad and at the end.
- Length is usually 1,200 to 2,000 characters.

## 7. Rules when writing in Voice B

**Take:** the two-line fact-and-twist opening, the mid-post pivot question, plain step-by-step explanation, one personal memory or habit, one zoom-out, a balanced tone, and a closing question about the reader.

**Never take:**
- His stories, memories, jobs, family or credentials. The personal line must come from the author the post is published under, or be left out.
- His sentences, sayings or sign-offs word for word.
- The sponsored-post format in post 6 unless the author has a real, disclosed partnership.
- Market calls, return promises or product verdicts. The author's compliance rules come first.
- Anything that could be mistaken for a post by Sharan Hegde.

**Conflicts with the post-writer rules, and which wins:**
- The twisted-saying opener ends in a question. The post-writer rule against a question as line 1 wins: keep the saying, end it on a statement.
- "There's even a name for this" and "And the irony is" are reveal bridges that the density rule limits. Use at most one per post.
