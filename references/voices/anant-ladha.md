## 2. Who he is (as reported)

- CA, CFA, CFP, LL.B. Founder of Invest Aaj For Kal, based in Kota, Rajasthan.
- Left a salaried job in Mumbai in 2016 to join the family broking and mutual fund distribution business.
- Ran 300+ free financial literacy sessions in Rajasthan before moving to YouTube.
- The channel teaches in Hindi and Hinglish; reported as India's most-viewed stock-market channel in 2022.
- Co-author of "13 Swing Trading Strategies" with his father.
- His channel states it is not a SEBI-registered investment adviser and that content is educational.

## 3. How he thinks (best-supported section)

1. **Ethics before money.** He credits this to his grandfather and repeats it when talking about clients.
2. **Care beats cleverness.** His claim is that others may be smarter but nobody will care more. Empathy is his stated edge.
3. **Free education first, business second.** He gives teaching away and treats trust as what later brings clients.
4. **Consistency over brilliance.** Daily publishing, including when unwell, is a point of pride.
5. **Act before it is perfect.** Start with a phone camera; improve later.
6. **Teach in the learner's language.** Small-town and first-time investors are the audience, so jargon is translated or dropped.
7. **Skills and active income come first.** In 2026 he said the first ₹1 crore is earned through active income and skills.
8. **Optimistic about India.** He frames the Indian market as a long-term growth story.
9. **Family first.** He says regrets follow otherwise.

## 4. How he writes on LinkedIn (inferred from fragments)

**Register**
- Conversational and blunt. Reads like speech typed out, including imperfect grammar.
- First person, direct address to "you".
- Mostly English with Hinglish lines dropped in for humour or warmth.
- Mild swearing appears when dismissing a common belief.

**Ways he opens (patterns, not his wording)**
- A flat dismissal of a popular belief.
- A dated personal moment: "In [year] when I started..." or a childhood admission.
- A pop-culture or news hook turned into a question.
- A numbered promise: "[N] rules you can't miss if you want to [outcome]".
- A big government or market number stated plainly.
- A cross-market comparison anchored in time: "[N] years back, X was at the level of Y."
- Two or three word prompts: a versus, or "Do you agree?"

**Body**
- Numbered points, often three.
- A parenthetical nudge to keep reading, such as telling the reader which point not to skip.
- Short paragraphs, one thought each.
- Personal stories about being doubted early and proving it through persistence.

**Close**
- A question back to the audience, often a rhetorical one that restates his opinion.
- Three to five broad hashtags (business, India, investment, money, life, success).

**Topic mix**
- Stock market rules and behaviour
- Mutual funds and SIPs
- Term and health insurance, emergency funds
- IPOs and market news
- His own journey and work ethic
- Workplace and career opinions
- Business commentary on popular shows

**Profile headline habit**
- Stacks social proof: subscriber counts, awards, talks, follower numbers.

## 6. Rules when writing in Voice B for Hasan

**Take**
- Blunt, spoken register and short paragraphs.
- Opening on a dismissed belief, a dated moment or a plain big number.
- Three numbered points with a reason to reach the last one.
- The values: care, consistency, education before selling.
- A closing question that carries an opinion.

**Never take**
- His stories, awards, numbers, family history or credentials. Hasan is a B.Com graduate, an AMFI-registered distributor and a CFA Level 1 candidate; nothing may imply otherwise.
- His exact sentences, catchphrases or sign-offs.
- "Money-making opportunity" framing, market calls, or asset-versus-asset verdicts. His channel carries its own disclaimer; Hasan's posts must stay as education under his ARN.
- Swearing, unless Hasan asks for it in a specific post.
- Posts that could be mistaken for being written by Anant Ladha.
